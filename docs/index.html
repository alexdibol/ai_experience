The conceptual foundation

What makes experience different?

Intelligence

The capacity to infer, compare, reason, plan, and propose actions.

Knowledge

The facts, relationships, constraints, and validated information on which reasoning depends.

Experience

Records of situated decisions and observed consequences, assessed for what they can teach a future decision maker.

A useful experience connects the situation, available evidence, action, outcome, assessment, validity conditions, and provenance. Its value depends on whether the lesson remains relevant.

Complementary approaches to institutional learning
ApproachContributionThe experience dimension
RAGRetrieves external evidence for a model to use in its response.Connects retrieved material to decisions, subsequent outcomes, and validated lessons.
Knowledge graphs and GraphRAGOrganize and retrieve relationships among entities, events, and evidence.Records which investigative paths were useful, under which conditions, and at what cost.
Second brainsPreserve and organize information for later use.Introduces explicit feedback, lesson review, and controlled revision of what influences decisions.
Fine-tuningAdapts model behavior by changing its parameters.Provides an additional learning pathway through external, inspectable memory; weight updates are not required for every new lesson.
Continual learningAddresses adaptation as new information and conditions arrive.Makes outcome evaluation, temporal validity, contradiction, and governance central to institutional adaptation.
The dynamic component is essential: improve the body of knowledge and the procedures used to reason with it. Updating external memory is distinct from continuously training model weights. Improvement must be measured, not assumed.

Build · Inspect · Compare

Four Google Colab laboratories.

Follow the progression from persistent evidence to a system that can challenge its own history. Each experiment isolates a mechanism and makes its limitations visible.

1 — Persistent memory

The Financial AI That Remembers

Stores synthetic credit episodes and lessons, retrieves relevant history, and compares decisions with and without memory. Introduces provenance, contradiction review, persistence, and evaluation.

Open notebook
2 — Remembered investigations

A Credit Committee with an Obsidian Memory

Combines a financial knowledge graph with remembered investigative journeys. Examines path choices, information costs, memory contamination, and an exportable Obsidian vault.

Open notebook
3 — Experience and reasoning

A Credit Committee That Uses Experience and Intelligence

Uses remembered experience to inform competing investigative branches. Compares architectures under controlled information budgets and examines whether memory improves the investigation.

4 — Belief revision

When to Remember, When to Forget

Introduces a changing synthetic market. Explores consolidation, contradiction, retirement of stale beliefs, adaptation lag, and the value or cost of memory.

Continuous experience

A governed learning cycle.

A credit decision may reveal weaknesses months later. An investigative path may fail in a new context. A market rule may become obsolete. Institutional learning must respond to those changes.

1 · Record

Preserve the situation, evidence, and decision.

2 · Observe

Connect actions to subsequent outcomes when available.

3 · Propose

Distill candidate lessons and identify supporting evidence.

4 · Validate

Review lessons and define where they apply.

5 · Retrieve

Use approved experience to inform later decisions.

6 · Revise

Challenge, update, or retire lessons as conditions change.

Preserve history. Reconsider its influence. Retain an episode for audit while withdrawing an obsolete lesson from active use.

Resilience · Safety · Productivity · Innovation

Questions for financial institutions.

Evidence

What was known at the time, and can its source be traced?

Validity

Does the lesson still apply to this borrower, regime, or decision?

Evaluation

Does memory improve decisions against a fair baseline, including costs and errors?

Security

Can untrusted material contaminate memory or redirect future behavior?

Governance

Who can propose, approve, challenge, revise, or retire a lesson?

Accountability

Can the institution reconstruct the decision and reverse an inappropriate update?
